## 1. RF — 2.4 GHz channel plan

### Why the location band is 2.4 GHz

The uplink governs (CLAUDE.md §2): location is computed from frames the
sensor hears **from** the cart panel at ~17 dBm EIRP
(`config: cart_uplink.eirp_dbm`), not from the AP's 24 dBm downlink.
On that budget, coverage at cart-panel height 1.00 m
(`config: heights.cart_panel_z_m`) is decided by gondola crossings —
17.7 dB per crossing at 2.4 GHz vs 33.4 / 36.7 dB at 5 / 6 GHz
(`config: attenuation_db`, ITU-R P.2040-3). The coverage runs bear it
out:

| band | sales ≥3 AP | sales blind | BOH ≥3 AP | BOH blind |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.4 | 99.5 % | 0.5 % | 95.4 % | 3.7 % |
| 5 | 99.5 % | 0.5 % | 66.9 % | 17.2 % |
| 6 | 99.3 % | 0.5 % | 66.0 % | 17.8 % |

(provenance: README.md "What the model shows", this parametric dataset,
2 m grid; the spec's surveyed model in CLAUDE.md §2 reports 95.5 / 66.0 /
64.7 % sales-floor ≥3 AP — same ordering, 2.4 GHz wins decisively in
both.) The 2 dB of extra antenna gain at 6 GHz
(`config: sensors.antenna_gain_dbi`: 4.0 / 5.0 / 6.0 dBi) does not begin
to pay for the extra ~19 dB per gondola. **All positioning-critical RF
planning below is for the 2.4 GHz radio.** 5/6 GHz remain for ops
traffic capacity and are RRM-managed.

### Why 20 MHz channels

At 2.4 GHz there are exactly **three non-overlapping 20 MHz channels: 1,
6, 11** (verified — IEEE 802.11 channelisation; 25 MHz centre spacing
2412/2437/2462 MHz > 20 MHz occupied width). 40 MHz bonding would
collapse the reuse plan to effectively one clean channel and is a
throughput feature, not a location feature: RTLS needs many sensors
*hearing* the panel (`config: rules.min_aps_for_position = 3` at
≥ −67 dBm, `config: rules.location_threshold_dbm`, Cisco Location
Deployment Guidelines), not wide pipes. 20 MHz maximises the number of
mutually non-interfering listeners per unit area. (assumed:
engineering judgment on the bonding trade-off; the channel count itself
is verified.)

### The plan

Generated by `scripts/plan_channels.py` (deterministic — no RNG; greedy
graph colouring in fixed `sensor_id` order; per sensor, pick the channel
minimising Σ 1/d² over already-assigned same-channel neighbours within
30 m, walls ignored = worst case; ties to the lowest channel). Output:
`data/channel_plan.json`. Both units of a diversity pair share their
logical position's channel — the pair is one listener, not two.

Results (provenance: `data/channel_plan.json`, stats block):

| metric | value |
|---|---|
| channel 1 / 6 / 11 counts | **50 / 50 / 50** |
| min same-channel spacing, global | **4.63 m** (pair S086–S096) |
| min same-channel spacing per channel | ch 1: 6.63 m · ch 6: 4.63 m · ch 11: 4.64 m |
| mean co-channel cost Σ 1/d² | 0.098769 m⁻² |
| max co-channel cost (worst sensor) | 0.177078 m⁻² |

**On the 4.63 m worst pair:** an 8 m same-channel floor is *provably
unachievable* on this layout — the sales grid contains 4 sensors
pairwise closer than 8 m (e.g. S018/S019/S028/S029, all six pairs
≤ 6.64 m), and with 3 channels the pigeonhole principle forces two of
any such 4 onto one channel. `tests/test_channel_plan.py` computes this
proof from the data and instead verifies the planner is the exact argmin
of its greedy rule at every step. The physical mitigation is not more
channels but the density itself: at 4.63 m the co-channel neighbour is a
*listener*, not a talker — sensors in monitor/scanning mode do not beacon
at cart-panel airtime scale, and the location threshold (−67 dBm) is an
uplink hearing rule, not a SINR rule. (assumed: engineering judgment.)

**RRM interaction:** RRM moves Tx power 2–26 dBm every 30 min
(`config: sensors.rrm`). Channels should be **statically pinned** to
this plan on the 2.4 GHz radio — a 30-minute channel churn would
invalidate the RSSI fingerprinting continuity Layer 3 depends on. Leave
RRM power adjustment on. (assumed: engineering judgment.)

## 2. PoE and switching

Per-unit PoE (provenance: `config: sensors.poe`, CW9172I — model
verified against datasheet, `config: sensors.model`):

| | per unit | × 300 units |
|---|---|---|
| min | 12.95 W | 3,885 W |
| typical | 12.2 W | **3,660 W** |
| max (all radios + scanning) | 25.5 W | **7,650 W** |
| class | 802.3at (PoE+) | — |

Design to **max**, not typical: Air Marshal containment and full-time
scanning radios are exactly the modes this deployment runs (CLAUDE.md
§5 gaps table), and 25.5 W is within 802.3at's 30 W per-port budget with
margin for cable loss.

### Switch count

- Ports: 300 units ÷ 48 ports = 6.25 → **7 access switches** (336
  ports, 36 spare for gates/POS/docks uplinks). (arithmetic from
  config values above.)
- Power per switch, worst case: ⌈300/7⌉ = 43 units × 25.5 W ≈
  **1,097 W PoE draw** — requires a UPOE-class 48-port switch with
  ≥ 1,100 W PoE budget, i.e. dual power supplies (e.g. 2 × 1,100 W,
  1+1). (assumed: engineering judgment; vendor PSU figures are not a
  repo input.)
- **N+1: 8 switches total.** Losing any one switch strands at most 43
  units = ~21 logical positions; with N+1 and paired units split across
  *different* switches per logical position (patching rule below), a
  single switch failure degrades diversity (−2.5 dB,
  `config: sensors.diversity_gain_db`) but never silences a logical
  position. (assumed: engineering judgment.)

**Patching rule:** the two units of each logical pair terminate on two
different switches. This converts every single-switch failure from "21
positions dark" into "43 positions lose 2.5 dB". (assumed: engineering
judgment.)

### Cabling

- Mount at 3.00 m (`config: sensors.mount_z_m`) below a 6.40 m ceiling
  (`config: envelope.ceiling_m`) → each drop is a **3.40 m pendant**
  (`config: sensors.pendant_drop_m`) from ceiling tray to sensor. Add
  3.4 m + service loop (~2 m, assumed) to every horizontal run.
- The envelope is 100 m × 55 m (`config: envelope.ring`). A single MDF
  cannot serve the far corner within the 100 m TIA-568 channel limit
  once tray routing, the 3.4 m drop and patch cords are added.
  **Two IDFs**: one on the sales side, one back-of-house, split at the
  x = 50.58 m wall (`config: areas.x_split_m`) — worst-case horizontal
  run then stays under ~75 m rectilinear. (assumed: engineering
  judgment; 100 m limit is TIA-568 standard.)
- Cat6A throughout: 802.3at at 25.5 W continuous benefits from the
  lower DC resistance and better heat dissipation in bundles.
  (assumed: engineering judgment.)

## 3. VLAN plan

| VLAN | name | carries | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | `mgmt` | AP/switch management, controller, telemetry POSTs | sensors' native VLAN on access trunks |
| 20 | `ops-wifi` | staff phones, handhelds (ops SSID) | staff phones associate here (`config: staffing.phone`) |
| 30 | `pos` | POS terminals, payment gateway | isolated; PCI scope — no wireless client egress |
| 40 | `cart-iot` | cart panel SSID | the uplink that matters; rate-limited, client-isolated |
| 50 | `guest` | guest portal | internet-only |

(VLAN IDs assumed: engineering judgment; the five-way split follows the
deliverable spec.)

### Why Air Marshal's `wiredMacs` depends on trunk visibility

`sensing/observation.py::air_marshal_record` (docstring): *"`wiredMacs`
is the field that separates a rogue attached to your network from one of
the five neighbour networks on this floor and above it. Without it the
false-positive rate is unusable."*

Air Marshal populates `wiredMacs`/`wiredVlans` by correlating BSSIDs
heard over the air with MAC addresses seen **on the wire** — and it can
only see wire-side MACs on VLANs that are actually trunked to the
switchports the APs sit on. Consequences:

- The AP access ports must be **trunks carrying all five VLANs**
  (10/20/30/40/50), even though the sensor itself only sources traffic
  on `mgmt` — otherwise a rogue AP plugged into, say, the POS VLAN is
  invisible to the wired correlation and gets classified as just another
  of the five neighbour networks (false negative on the one VLAN that
  matters most).
- The `air_marshal_record` also emits `wiredVlans`: with the full trunk,
  a hit tells you *which* segment the rogue touched — triage on VLAN 30
  is a different incident than VLAN 50.
- Cost: trunking POS to the ceiling widens PCI scope. Mitigate with
  switchport BPDU-guard + port-security on all access ports and by
  keeping VLAN 30 pruned from ports that host no AP. (assumed:
  engineering judgment.)
- Operational caveat from the model: when containment is active the
  scanning radio time-splits and RTLS accuracy degrades (CLAUDE.md §5
  gaps table) — containment stays manual, consistent with the project's
  `RECOMMEND_ONLY` posture (CLAUDE.md §6).

## 4. Telemetry transport

### Scanning API v3 POSTs

Delivery is batched HTTP POSTs at a **jittered 60–180 s interval, not
guaranteed** (provenance: `config: pipeline.post_interval_s = [60, 180]`,
implemented in `sensing/pipeline.py` — "POST interval 60-180 s,
jittered, not guaranteed"; 2 % record loss,
`config: pipeline.record_loss_prob`). Sizing on the mgmt VLAN:

- Population per batch: ~200 carts + staff phones sampled 1-in-3 ticks
  (`config: staffing.phone.observe_every_n_ticks`); unassociated
  randomised MACs never reach the API (`sensing/pipeline.py`), which
  *shrinks* the payload — do not size for every shopper phone.
  (population count assumed: engineering judgment.)
- A `DevicesSeen` record with `locations[]` + `rssiRecords[]` for ~10
  hearing APs is ~1.5 kB JSON (assumed: engineering judgment from the
  record shape in `sensing/observation.py::devices_seen_record`).
  ~250 records / batch ≈ 375 kB per POST; at the worst-case 60 s
  interval that is **~50 kbit/s sustained** — negligible on any uplink,
  but the receiver must tolerate the jitter: Layer 3 treats "no POST for
  180 s" as *not observed*, never as *absent* (CLAUDE.md §5).
- Sign convention: `DevicesSeen` positive vs `BluetoothDevicesSeen`
  negative — normalised on ingest and recorded
  (`sensing/observation.py`, `dlai/ingest.py`).

### aWIPS syslog

- Carries **no client identity** and is throttled to **one message per
  signature per AP per interval**, so attack intensity is lost
  (provenance: CLAUDE.md §5 gaps table; `sensing/aireye.py`). Size for
  the ceiling, not the attack: 150 logical positions × ~20 signatures ×
  1 msg/interval ≈ 3,000 msgs per throttle interval worst case; at
  ~300 B/syslog line that is < 1 MB per interval — **any single syslog
  collector on VLAN 10 absorbs it**. (signature count and line size
  assumed: engineering judgment.)
- Because intensity is destroyed at the source, the collector must alarm
  on *presence* of a signature, not message rate. (design consequence
  of the throttling gap, CLAUDE.md §5.)
- Transport: UDP/514 to a collector reachable only on `mgmt`; no client
  MACs are present in these messages, so this stream is not PII-scoped.
  (assumed: engineering judgment.)
